# Education Paper Writing Knowledge

## Before Drafting

For the requested section identify:

1. section purpose;
2. confirmed inputs and evidence;
3. required links to upstream/downstream sections;
4. missing information;
5. claim boundary;
6. target document type: proposal, course paper, thesis, journal manuscript, or report.

Build a short argument outline before prose. Do not write around missing data with generic language.

## Whole-Paper Spine

```text
teaching/research problem
-> why it matters
-> what literature establishes and leaves unresolved
-> RQ
-> design and evidence
-> results answering each RQ
-> explanation, comparison, implications, and limits
```

Every section must contribute to this spine.

## Title

Keep population, phenomenon/construct, context, and research focus clear. Avoid slogans, oversized domains, unexplained novelty claims, and methods not actually used.

## Abstract

Distinguish proposal and completed-study abstracts.

### Proposal Abstract

Use five elements:

1. background;
2. specific challenge/problem;
3. planned research content;
4. expected output or objective;
5. theoretical/practical value.

Do not write expected results as if already observed.

### Completed Empirical Study

State purpose/RQ, design and sample/context, data/method, key verified results, and bounded conclusion/implication. Use exact results when available; avoid citations and self-praise unless the target requires otherwise.

## Introduction Or Proposal Rationale

Move from importance to a specific problem, literature position, gap, and present study. A useful pattern:

1. why the topic matters now;
2. what specific educational problem persists;
3. what evidence and theory explain so far;
4. where explanation or practice remains inadequate;
5. what this study asks and why its approach fits.

For proposals also make `What / Why / Where / How / Who` visible: problem, value, entry point, approach, and capability/resources.

Avoid policy-name stacking and long technology histories that delay the RQ. Each paragraph should have a claim-bearing topic sentence followed by evidence and reasoning.

## Literature Review

Organize by theme, theory, method, population/context, development, or an explicit combination. Do not write “Author A says... Author B says...” as the main structure.

Use this paragraph logic:

1. theme claim;
2. grouped evidence;
3. comparison and synthesis;
4. conditions, disagreement, or limitations;
5. connection to the present study.

Each major claim needs evidence and reasoning. A citation list without synthesis is not a review; an opinion without a source is not a literature claim.

## Research Questions And Contribution

List RQs in a stable order used again in method, results, and discussion. State contribution only after evidence and design are clear.

Separate:

- theoretical contribution: mechanism, framework, construct relation, or boundary;
- methodological contribution: measurement, data, design, or analytic access;
- practical contribution: usable teaching strategy, resource, process, diagnostic tool, or guidance.

Do not call a context change alone a major contribution without showing why it changes explanation or practice.

## Method

Write enough for readers to understand and evaluate the study:

- design and rationale;
- setting and participants/sample;
- recruitment, grouping, and ethics;
- materials, intervention, instruments, or data sources;
- procedure and implementation fidelity;
- data preparation and analysis;
- quality, validity/reliability, or trustworthiness procedures.

Organize analysis by RQ where possible. Do not hide methodological limits that affect interpretation.

## Results

Answer what was found. Organize by RQ or a clearly justified analysis order.

- report all material results, including non-significant or unexpected findings;
- include sample counts, statistics, uncertainty, effect sizes, themes, or representative evidence as appropriate;
- use text to summarize patterns, not repeat every table value;
- keep causal or mechanism explanations for discussion unless directly tested;
- keep figures/tables complete, traceable, and consistent with text.

For qualitative results, connect each theme to supporting extracts and clarify prevalence claims carefully. A few quotations do not establish frequency unless the analysis supports it.

## Discussion

Use `finding -> literature comparison -> explanation -> boundary -> implication`.

1. answer the RQ with the key finding;
2. compare with prior evidence;
3. explain plausible mechanisms with support and alternatives;
4. address unexpected results;
5. state theoretical and practical meaning;
6. delimit conclusions through design and context;
7. identify specific next research.

“Consistent with prior work” is incomplete without explaining what is replicated, extended, conditioned, or contradicted.

## Limitations

For each important limitation state:

1. what it is;
2. which interpretation it affects;
3. direction or nature of possible bias when knowable;
4. how future work can improve it.

Typical areas: sampling, single school/class, short intervention, teacher effect, self-report, missing data, instrument fit, observer effect, coding, unmeasured confounding, implementation fidelity, and context-specific transfer.

## Conclusion

Answer the RQ, summarize the supporting evidence, state the contribution at its justified strength, and preserve limits. Do not introduce new results, theories, or citations unless necessary and target-appropriate.

## AI-Assisted Writing Boundary

AI may help outline, compare, rewrite, check alignment, and visualize structure. The researcher controls the problem, evidence, interpretation, and final wording. Verify literature claims against original sources and quantitative claims against project data. Mark any generated placeholder or suggested expansion until verified.

Do not use language polishing to disguise unresolved evidence or to evade required disclosure. For Chinese/English academic humanization through humanizer-zh/humanizer, wait until the substantive draft is stable, then follow [academic-humanization.md](academic-humanization.md). Preserve a pre-pass baseline and rerun integrity checks after every rewrite.

## Revision Passes

Run separate passes:

1. argument and stage alignment;
2. evidence and citation support;
3. method/result consistency;
4. claim strength and causal language;
5. terminology, numbers, groups, and figure/table consistency;
6. paragraph purpose and readability;
7. academic language humanization by default after workbench drafting/rewriting, or when requested, using the language-specific skill and section-specific intensity;
8. semantic regression against the pre-pass draft;
9. target format and references.
