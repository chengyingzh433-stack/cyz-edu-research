# Education Research Methods Knowledge

## Core Alignment

Maintain this chain:

```text
practice phenomenon -> research problem -> literature position -> RQ
-> evidence needed -> design -> sample/data/tool -> analysis
-> result -> interpretation -> bounded conclusion
```

If one link does not support the next, repair the upstream decision before drafting downstream prose.

## From Concern To RQ

Distinguish:

- direction: broad field;
- topic: bounded phenomenon;
- RQ: answerable with observable evidence;
- hypothesis: testable expected relationship or difference when appropriate.

Check every RQ for:

- clear population and context;
- defined constructs;
- empirical answerability and potential falsification;
- theoretical or practical value;
- connection to existing knowledge;
- feasible data and ethics;
- manageable scope.

Question types and typical evidence:

| Type | Typical wording | Useful evidence/design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Descriptive | What is happening; what forms occur? | survey, observation, logs, interviews |
| Relational | How are A and B related or predictive? | survey/records, correlation/regression with cautions |
| Difference | How do groups or conditions differ? | comparison, quasi-experiment, qualitative comparison |
| Explanatory | Why/how/through what mechanism? | interviews, process data, cases, mixed methods |
| Intervention | Does X improve Y, for whom and under what conditions? | experiment/quasi-experiment, implementation evidence |
| Developmental/action | How can practice be improved through cycles? | action research, design-based research, iterative evidence |

## Topic Quality

Evaluate value, clarity, novelty, and feasibility. Novelty may come from theory, mechanism, population, context, method, measurement, time, or integration. “Nobody has done this” is neither required nor automatically valuable.

For practice-based topics, align:

1. observable problem;
2. two or three concrete difficulties;
3. proposed theory, strategy, or tool for each difficulty;
4. measurable or interpretable outcomes;
5. advantage over current practice.

## Design Selection

Choose methods from the evidence need, not personal familiarity.

### Quasi-Experiment

Use when comparing an intervention in natural classes where individual random assignment is unavailable. Specify groups, pre/post measures, intervention, comparison condition, teacher/content/time controls, implementation fidelity, clustering, confounders, attrition, and causal limits.

Randomly choosing which intact class receives treatment does not create individual randomization. Keep causal language proportionate.

### Survey

Use for prevalence, attitudes, behaviors, constructs, group differences, or relationships. Define the population, sampling, construct, instrument source, adaptation, pilot, administration, response, missing data, reliability, validity, and analysis.

### Interview

Use for experience, meaning, mechanism, reasoning, and conditions. Questions must be clear, single, non-leading, and answerable. Pilot the guide; report recruitment, setting, duration, recording, transcription, coding, researcher role, and evidence for themes.

### Observation

Use for real classroom behavior and process. Operationalize behaviors, time units, observation rules, observer training, recording, rechecking, and inter-rater consistency where relevant.

### Case Study

Define case boundaries, selection logic, context, multiple evidence sources, analytic strategy, rival explanations, and transfer conditions. Do not generalize statistically from a single case without justification.

### Action Research

Document problem diagnosis, plan, action, observation, reflection, and subsequent cycles. Separate practitioner goals from research claims. Use evidence across cycles and make reflexivity explicit.

### Mixed Methods

State why one method is insufficient, the priority and sequence of strands, sampling relation, integration point, and how joint interpretation is produced. More methods do not automatically mean better research.

## Sampling And Participants

Report target population, accessible population, setting, sampling frame, inclusion/exclusion, recruitment, sample size rationale, group allocation, attrition, participant characteristics, and limits on representativeness.

For class/school data, respect nested structure. A large student count from very few classes or schools does not equal broad independent sampling.

## Instruments And Data Sources

For each instrument or source record:

- construct and purpose;
- source/development and adaptation;
- items/tasks/coding unit;
- response/score and interpretation;
- evidence of validity and reliability or qualitative trustworthiness;
- pilot and revisions;
- administration/collection procedure;
- limitations in the present context.

Correct terminology: `validity = 效度`, `reliability = 信度`.

## RQ-Evidence-Analysis Alignment

Complete [research-design.md](../templates/research-design.md) before collecting data.

For every RQ specify:

- construct/phenomenon;
- data source;
- collection time and procedure;
- analysis method;
- expected result form;
- what conclusion the design can and cannot support.

Data with no RQ purpose signals loss of focus. An RQ with no evidence source signals an incomplete design.

## Analysis Planning

Choose analysis only after identifying design, variable/data type, independence or nesting, distribution/assumptions, missing data, and inferential target.

Quantitative work should normally report descriptive results, uncertainty, effect size, and assumptions in addition to significance tests. Complex models are justified by the RQ and data structure, not prestige.

Qualitative work must name and describe the analysis path. For thematic analysis, document familiarization, coding, theme development, review, definition/naming, and reporting with evidence. Preserve contradictory or negative cases.

Mixed-method analysis must show where results are merged, connected, embedded, or compared.

## Ethics

Before data collection address:

- ethics review or institutional requirements;
- informed consent and assent;
- guardian consent for minors when required;
- voluntariness and power relations between teacher and student;
- privacy, anonymization, secure storage, retention, and access;
- recording and image permissions;
- risks, burdens, compensation, withdrawal, and support;
- responsible AI use and protection of student data.

Ethics is part of design, not a sentence added after the study.
